SOCIAL NEEDS SCREENING PROTOCOLS
Implementation Guide for Medicare Advantage Plans

==================================================
OVERVIEW
==================================================

Social Needs Screening: Systematic assessment of members' non-medical barriers to health (food, housing, transportation, safety, utilities).

Why It Matters:
- CMS requirement for MA plans (2024+)
- Star Ratings measure (Screening for Social Needs)
- Drives disparity reduction
- Improves health outcomes and lowers costs

==================================================
VALIDATED SCREENING TOOLS
==================================================

1. PRAPARE (Protocol for Responding to and Assessing Patients' Assets, Risks, and Experiences)
   - 21 questions covering 7 domains
   - Used by FQHCs nationally
   - Available in 20+ languages

2. AHC-HRSN (Accountable Health Communities Health-Related Social Needs)
   - 10-question screener
   - CMS-tested in AHC Model
   - Focus on 5 core domains

3. CMS Standardized Screening Tool
   - 5 questions (food, housing, transport, utilities, safety)
   - Minimum for Star Ratings reporting
   - Quick administration (2-3 minutes)

==================================================
CORE SCREENING DOMAINS
==================================================

1. Food Insecurity
   Question: "Within the past 12 months, did you worry that your food would run out before you got money to buy more?"
   Follow-up: Frequency, current status

2. Housing Instability
   Question: "Are you worried about losing your housing?"
   Follow-up: Current living situation, eviction risk

3. Transportation Barriers
   Question: "In the past 12 months, has lack of transportation kept you from medical appointments, meetings, work, or getting things needed for daily living?"
   Follow-up: Specific barriers (no car, no public transit, cost)

4. Utility Assistance Needs
   Question: "In the past 12 months, has the electric, gas, oil, or water company threatened to shut off services?"
   Follow-up: Current status, payment plan

5. Interpersonal Safety
   Question: "Do you feel physically and emotionally safe where you currently live?"
   Follow-up: Intimate partner violence screening if indicated

==================================================
IMPLEMENTATION WORKFLOW
==================================================

Step 1: Identify Screening Opportunities
- Annual Wellness Visit (AWV)
- Health Risk Assessment (HRA)
- Post-discharge follow-up calls
- New member welcome calls
- Chronic disease management encounters

Step 2: Administer Screen
- Trained staff (nurse, social worker, CHW)
- Private setting (HIPAA compliant)
- Trauma-informed approach
- Member's preferred language

Step 3: Document Results
- Record in EHR with structured data fields
- Flag member chart for positive screens
- Report to care team

Step 4: Develop Intervention Plan
- For each positive screen: Specific intervention
- Timeline for resolution
- Assign responsible party

Step 5: Connect to Resources
- Make warm hand-off to community partner
- Provide written list of resources
- Schedule follow-up to confirm connection

Step 6: Track Outcomes
- Did member connect to resource?
- Was need resolved?
- Time to resolution

==================================================
INTERVENTION STRATEGIES BY DOMAIN
==================================================

Food Insecurity:
- SNAP application assistance
- Food pantry referrals (United Way 211)
- Meals on Wheels for homebound
- Medically tailored meals (MTM) post-discharge
- WIC for pregnant women

Housing Instability:
- Housing authority referrals
- Rent assistance programs
- Homeless services coordination
- Transitional housing
- Legal aid for eviction defense

Transportation:
- Non-emergency medical transportation (NEMT)
- Uber Health / Lyft vouchers ($25-50 per ride)
- Public transit passes
- Volunteer driver programs
- Telehealth as alternative

Utility Assistance:
- LIHEAP (Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program)
- Utility company payment plans
- Weatherization assistance
- Emergency utility assistance funds

Interpersonal Safety:
- Domestic violence hotline
- Safe shelter referrals
- Legal advocacy services
- Safety planning

==================================================
COMMUNITY PARTNERSHIPS
==================================================

Essential Partners:
- United Way 211 (centralized social services referral)
- Area Agencies on Aging (AAA)
- Community Health Centers (FQHCs)
- Food banks (Feeding America network)
- Public housing authorities
- Legal aid organizations
- Domestic violence shelters

Partnership Models:
1. Referral Only: Provide list of resources, member self-navigates
2. Assisted Referral: Staff makes call, schedules appointment
3. Warm Hand-Off: Direct connection, staff stays on call
4. Embedded Services: Community partners on-site at health center
5. Integrated Care: Social workers employed by health plan

==================================================
DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

For Star Ratings Credit:
- Screen at least once per year
- Use validated tool (PRAPARE, AHC-HRSN, or CMS tool)
- Document results in structured format
- For positive screens: Document intervention offered
- Report aggregated results to CMS

Required Data Fields:
- Date of screening
- Tool used
- Results by domain (positive/negative)
- Intervention plan (if positive)
- Referral made (yes/no)
- Member accepted referral (yes/no)
- Follow-up date scheduled

==================================================
STAR RATINGS MEASURE SPECIFICATIONS
==================================================

Numerator: Members screened for social needs
Denominator: All active members
Target: >80% of members screened annually
Star Threshold: 4 stars = 85%+, 5 stars = 90%+

Exclusions: Members termed <6 months

Additional Credit:
- Screen-positive rate documentation
- Intervention completion rate
- Need resolution rate

==================================================
WORKFLOW EXAMPLE: POST-DISCHARGE CALL
==================================================

Scenario: Member discharged from hospital for heart failure

Call Script:
1. "We want to make sure you have everything you need at home. I'm going to ask a few questions about any challenges you might be facing."

2. Food: "Do you have enough healthy food at home?"
   - If NO: "We can connect you with a program that delivers meals. Would you like that?"

3. Housing: "Is your housing situation stable? Do you have concerns about losing your home?"
   - If YES concerned: "Let me connect you with a housing counselor who can help."

4. Transportation: "Do you have a way to get to your follow-up doctor appointment next week?"
   - If NO: "We can arrange a free ride for you. What day works best?"

5. Medications: "Were you able to pick up your new heart medications?"
   - If NO: "Let's figure out what's preventing that. Is it cost, transportation, or something else?"

6. Safety: "Do you feel safe where you're living?"
   - If NO: Offer resources based on concern

Document all responses, make referrals, schedule follow-ups.

==================================================
COMMON CHALLENGES AND SOLUTIONS
==================================================

Challenge: Members reluctant to disclose needs
Solution: 
- Frame as "routine questions we ask everyone"
- Explain confidentiality
- Use non-judgmental language
- Ask permission: "Is it okay if I ask about..."

Challenge: No community resources available
Solution:
- Build resource directory in advance
- Partner with multiple organizations per domain
- Offer plan-funded alternatives (Uber vouchers, meals)
- Advocate for new resources (present data to county)

Challenge: Members don't follow through on referrals
Solution:
- Warm hand-offs, not cold referrals
- Simplify process (one-stop shop)
- Provide incentives ($25 gift card for completing connection)
- Assign navigator to accompany member

Challenge: Resource capacity maxed out
Solution:
- Develop tiering system (most urgent first)
- Fund expansion of high-demand services
- Create alternatives (telehealth vs transportation)

MEASURING SUCCESS
==================================================

Process Metrics:
- % members screened
- % screen-positive
- % positive screens receiving intervention
- % referrals completed

Outcome Metrics:
- % needs resolved within 90 days
- Reduction in ER visits for screen-positive members
- Improvement in quality measure compliance
- Member satisfaction scores

Financial Metrics:
- Cost per screening: $15-30
- Cost per intervention: $50-500
- Avoided medical costs: $500-$3,000 per resolved need
- ROI: 300-600%

BEST PRACTICES
==================================================

✅ Universal Screening (not targeted)
✅ Use validated tools
✅ Train staff on trauma-informed care
✅ Offer in member's preferred language
✅ Make warm referrals, not handouts
✅ Follow up to confirm connection
✅ Track outcomes, not just process
✅ Partner with trusted community organizations
✅ Integrate with quality improvement programs
✅ Report results to leadership monthly

KEY TAKEAWAYS
==================================================

Social needs screening is now a CMS requirement and Star Ratings measure. Successful programs:

✅ Screen universally and systematically
✅ Use validated tools
✅ Make warm referrals to trusted partners
✅ Track outcomes, not just process
✅ Integrate with quality improvement

Addressing social needs improves health outcomes, quality measures, and reduces costs. It's both the right thing to do and good business.
